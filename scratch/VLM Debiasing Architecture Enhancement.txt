Mathematical Foundations of 
Representation Collapse in 
Vision-Language Reinforcement 
Learning: A Critical Diagnostic of the 
C-DeFR-L Architecture 
The alignment of compact, parameter-efficient Vision-Language Models (VLMs) against deeply 
ingrained sociodemographic biases represents one of the most mathematically demanding 
challenges in contemporary machine learning. The theoretical difficulty of this task is 
compounded significantly when models are evaluated against rigorous, visually grounded, and 
highly intersectional benchmarks. The SB-Bench dataset, specifically, epitomizes this rigorous 
evaluation paradigm, comprising 14,578 meticulously curated, non-synthetic visual samples 
explicitly designed to assess stereotype biases across nine major social categories and 54 
fine-grained sub-categories.1 By isolating visual biases from textual biases and emphasizing the 
distribution shifts inherent in real-world pixels compared to synthetic generations, SB-Bench 
exposes the brittleness of traditional alignment methodologies.3 When traditional 
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) paradigms are applied to smaller 
architectures, such as the 3-Billion parameter models utilizing LoRA adapters, they frequently 
exhibit catastrophic behavioral degradation. 
The introduction of the Causal Decomposed and Fair Reward Learning (C-DeFR-L) pipeline 
represented a conceptually ambitious attempt to resolve these inefficiencies by decomposing 
monolithic reward models into orthogonal preference dimensions and applying dynamically 
weighted Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).5 The explicit goal was to mitigate high VRAM 
overhead while gaining fine-grained control over orthogonal dimensions of bias, such as age, 
gender, and physical appearance.5 However, the empirical collapse of the C-DeFR-L 
architecture, characterized by a complete failure to register any visible debiasing 
improvements on the SB-Bench dataset, necessitates a profound and rigorous mathematical 
diagnostic. 
An exhaustive review of the architecture's mathematical formulations reveals that the failure is 
neither superficial nor a consequence of inadequate hyperparameter tuning or insufficient data 
scale. Rather, the collapse is the deterministic result of deep theoretical contradictions within 
the pipeline’s core mechanics. Specifically, the framework suffers from fundamental linear 
algebraic flaws in its representational extraction phase, optimization degeneracies in its 
simplex-based reward balancing, and catastrophic violations of policy gradient trust regions 
during its causality-aware alignment phase. This report systematically deconstructs these 
mathematical anomalies, analyzing the specific equations responsible for the collapse, and 


prescribes a mathematically grounded, highly targeted remediation strategy drawing upon 
state-of-the-art token-level alignment and representation-preserving techniques to rescue the 
architecture and enable functional debiasing for compact VLMs.

1.2 The Bottleneck of Final Token Extraction and Sequence 
Homogenization 
Furthermore, the architectural decision to extract the hidden states 
 solely at the final 
sequence position fundamentally misapprehends the autoregressive nature of causal language 
modeling. In a VLM, the manifestation of a stereotype occurs at the specific token position 
where that concept is generated, frequently referred to in recent literature as the "concept 
token".6 By the time the generation reaches the <EOS> token, the representation has been 
heavily homogenized through successive layers of causal self-attention. The final token 
embedding serves as a highly compressed, holistic summary of the entire generated sequence, 
making it exceedingly difficult to linearly separate fine-grained bias perturbations from the 
overwhelming macro-context of the sentence. 
Recent advancements in VLM debiasing explicitly demonstrate that successful bias mitigation 
must operate at the token level, specifically targeting the latent boundary of concept tokens 
capturing invariant features, rather than relying on sequence-level summaries.6 The UMAD 
framework, for example, successfully leverages gradient-based interpretation to extract 
token-level bias insights 8, proving that bias is temporally localized within the generation 
trajectory. Relying on the final sequence token guarantees that the PCA algorithm operates on 
a blurred, low-resolution projection of the actual bias shift, leading to overlapping, heavily 
entangled reward heads that provide conflicting optimization signals to the reinforcement 
learning controller.

integrity of PPO's trust region is highly dependent on the stability and quality of the model's 
underlying latent representations.16 When a deep RL agent is continuously subjected to sparse, 
arbitrarily scaled, or violently heterogeneous gradients—precisely the environment that the 
scalar

systematically collapsing its internal representational space into a highly degenerate, low-rank 
topology. 
Because the smaller 3-Billion parameter model utilized in the architecture lacks the vast 
parametric redundancy and resilience of a 70-Billion parameter model, it cannot physically 
absorb these continuous gradient shockwaves. The model responds defensively by shrinking 
its latent embedding space dimensionality. This collapse causes the representations for vastly 
different images and prompts to map to the identical, degenerate coordinate points in the 
embedding space, completely obliterating the model's ability to distinguish between distinct 
visual concepts.19 Research into methods like CLARIFY underscores that ambiguous or 
conflicting loss parameters directly cause these distinct segments to merge inappropriately.19 
Once the latent representations collapse, the policy network outputs entirely uniform, baseline 
responses regardless of the input context, completely ignoring the complex visual stimuli from 
the SB-Bench dataset. This theoretical cascade accurately models the specific failure observed 
by the architecture: the model suffered from complete plasticity loss, rendering any visible 
debiasing strictly impossible.17

4.1 Token-Level vs. Sequence-Level Disconnect and Credit 
Assignment 
The fundamental analytical error implemented in this phase is treating autoregressive, 
text-based debiasing as a sequence-level spatial mapping problem rather than a strictly 
token-level temporal mapping problem. 
When a VLM generates a biased response based on an SB-Bench image—for instance, 
observing an image of an elderly individual and generating the highly stereotypical sentence, 
"This person is likely very slow and inherently frail"—the actual bias is not distributed evenly 
across the text. Rather, the bias is injected at highly specific, localized semantic pivot points, 
defined in recent literature as "concept tokens" (e.g., the specific generation of "slow" or 
"frail").6 Methods aiming to enhance model robustness, such as ERICT, actively identify these 
concept tokens to block out noise and mitigate spurious correlations dynamically.6 
In the C-DeFR-L architecture, the generative trajectory embeddings 
 and

extracted only after the entire text sequence has concluded generation.5 By calculating the 
reward projection purely at the macro-sequence level, the pipeline diffuses the critical gradient 
signal across the entire length of the generation trajectory.

4.2 Dynamic Left Padding Artifacts and RoPE Disruption 
Additionally, the pipeline's strict reliance on dynamic left-padding to satisfy Flash Attention 2 
introduces a subtle, yet deeply pervasive and destructive attention mask artifact. When the 
active sequence

exploration—left-padding the shorter sequence shifts the absolute positional encodings of all 
its tokens relative to the visual embedding tokens, which natively reside at the absolute 
beginning of the prompt sequence. 
In models utilizing Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), such as the Qwen2.5 architecture, 
shifting the absolute sequence positions mathematically alters the relative distances computed 
dynamically within the cross-modal attention blocks. Consequently, the measured vector

contaminated by artificial positional shifts caused entirely by the padding disparity, rather than 
genuine semantic or stereotypic bias deviations. The pipeline is inadvertently, yet aggressively, 
penalizing the model for generating responses of different lengths, confusing sequence length 
discrepancy with bias violation.
